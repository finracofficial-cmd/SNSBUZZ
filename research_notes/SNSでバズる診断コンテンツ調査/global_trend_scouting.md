# Global Viral UGC Web Tools, Quizzes, Generators and AI "Self-Discovery" Formats (2025 – Oct 2026): Scouting for Japan Localization (Ages 10s–20s)

Research date: 2026-10-23. Scope: US / Europe / Korea / China / Taiwan / SEA (plus India where it drove global AI-image trends). Items before 2025 are marked **[PRECEDENT]**. Every fact has an inline source. Anything uncited is in Inferences or Gaps. Where sources conflict, both are given.

Method note: the web-search tool is US-centric. Japanese- and Chinese-language queries returned thinner results, so Japan "reach" evidence comes mostly from Japanese media explainers, not platform metrics. TikTok Creative Center and Google Trends could not be accessed (no direct data pulled).

---

## Q1+Q2. Which formats went viral overseas in 2025–2026, and what are their mechanics, share design, scale, lifespan and monetization?

### Takeaway
The biggest 2025–2026 waves fall into four families. (1) **"Upload a selfie → AI restyles you" image prompts**: Ghibli (Mar 2025), action figure / starter pack (Apr 2025), Nano Banana figurine / polaroid / hug-your-younger-self (Sep 2025), ChatGPT caricature (Feb 2026). (2) **"Ask the AI what it knows about you" memory-mirror prompts**: roast me, "how I treat you", "Your Year with ChatGPT". (3) **Meme-label typology quizzes that replace MBTI**: Korea's テト/エゲン (2025), China's SBTI (Apr 2026). (4) **Data-recap "Wrapped" cards**: Spotify Wrapped 2025 "Listening Age", NetEase joint annual report. Nearly all the AI-image waves ran on free consumer AI apps, not standalone web tools, and peaked within 1–3 weeks. The quiz-type waves (SBTI) were made by individuals, ran as no-login web pages, and spread purely by screenshots.

### Cited Findings

#### A. AI image style-transfer / "toy-ification" trends (platform-native, free consumer AI)

**A1. Ghibli-style portraits (ChatGPT GPT-4o image gen), late Mar 2025**
- GPT-4o image generation launched for paid users on March 25, 2025. Free users got access around April 1 with a 3-images/day limit — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/openai-chatgpt-ghibli-image-tool-surge-users-1m-hour-altman-says/)
- Sam Altman posted on Mar 31, 2025 that ChatGPT "added one million users in the last hour" and asked users to "chill on generating images". This is Altman's own claim and has not been independently verified — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/openai-chatgpt-ghibli-image-tool-surge-users-1m-hour-altman-says/)
- OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap reportedly said 130M+ users generated 700M+ images in the first week, and that India became ChatGPT's fastest-growing market because of the feature. This comes from secondary Spanish-language reports; the original X post was not found — [El Espectador](https://www.elespectador.com/tecnologia/usuarios-de-chatgpt-generaron-mas-de-700-millones-de-imagenes-en-una-semana/); [Hipertextual](https://hipertextual.com/internet/chatgpt-rompe-record-imagenes-studio-ghibli/)
- Similarweb: ChatGPT average weekly active users passed 150M for the first time that year during the surge — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1106406)
- **Japan reach (confirmed):** after free-tier access opened, Japanese SNS filled with Ghibli-style self-portraits (ITmedia). Studio Ghibli replied "no particular comment", and a fake "Ghibli warning letter" circulated and was denied — [ITmedia](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/article/2504/11/1250411144/). Lawyer 福井健策: imitating a style is generally not copyright infringement in most countries, but whether training on copies is lawful varies by country — [弁護士ドットコム](https://www.bengo4.com/c_23/n_18630/). One report says direct "ジブリ風" prompts were sometimes internally rewritten — [AIsmiley](https://aismiley.co.jp/ai_news/chatgpt-ghibli/)

**A2. AI action figure / "starter pack" (ChatGPT 4o), Apr 2025**
- Mechanic: upload a photo plus a prompt asking for a blister-pack toy with accessories that reflect your job and personality. It spread across TikTok, X, Facebook and LinkedIn within about a week. Brands joined (Starbucks, NYX Cosmetics), and a counter-hashtag **#StarterPackNoAI** appeared among artists — [Fast Company ME](https://fastcompanyme.com/?p=38137)
- The origin is unclear. The free tier allowed 3 image uses per day — [Blavity](https://blavity.com/chatgpt-action-figure-prompt); [Gazette](https://www.gazette-news.co.uk/news/25083963.chatgpt-action-figure-trend---make-ai-doll)

**A3. Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) 1/7-scale figurine, early Sep 2025 → offshoots**
- Google's Josh Woodward: 200M+ images edited and 10M+ people new to the Gemini app shortly after launch — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/04/gemini-app-nano-banana/). On Sep 8 he posted that Gemini had added 13M more first-time users (23M+ total) and 500M+ images in total — [Decrypt (ES)](https://decrypt.co/es/339106/como-usar-la-ia-nano-banana-de-google-para-transformar-tus-selfies-en-figuras-digitales/)
- By mid-September the Gemini iPhone app was the #1 free app in the US — [American Bazaar](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2025/09/15/google-geminis-nano-banana-trend-surpasses-openai-tops-apple-app-store-467610/)
- India was the #1 country for Nano Banana usage, with a local twist: retro Bollywood portraits and the "AI saree" look — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/india-leads-the-way-on-googles-nano-banana-with-a-local-creative-twist)
- Mechanic: photo plus a "1/7 scale commercialized figurine" prompt, with packaging, stand and 3D-modeling-screen props. The tool was free and needed no design skills — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/explained-the-nano-banana-trend-driving-viral-3d-figurines-across-social-media-80860.htm)
- Offshoots in Sep–Oct 2025: celebrity polaroids, "Hug My Younger Self" (upload a current photo plus a childhood photo), and Ghostface Y2K Halloween portraits — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/how-to-follow-hug-my-younger-self-trend-instagrams-latest-viral-ai-fad-80989.htm); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/shashi-tharoor-embraces-22-year-old-self-in-viral-ai-hug-trend-1.500282845); [PhotoGPT blog, low reliability](https://www.photogptai.com/blog/ai-photo-trends-2026)
- Dec 2025: a Nano Banana Pro trend of "3D floating country island" posters — [Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/digital/how-to-create-viral-3d-floating-country-islands-using-googles-nano-banana-ai-85887.htm)
- Unverified claim: Sundar Pichai said Nano Banana models generated 50B+ images, "the largest single-model trend on record". This is reported only by a vendor blog — [PhotoGPT](https://www.photogptai.com/blog/ai-photo-trends-2026)
- Safety backlash: Indian police and experts warned about scams and data risk from uploading selfies — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/trending/trendy-meets-tricky-banana-ai-saree-trend-raises-cybersecurity-red-flags/amp)
- **Japan reach (confirmed):** spread on X and Instagram under #NanoBanana and #フィギュア化. Google Japan itself posted a figurine example on Sep 16, 2025. Japanese guides note the model is weak at rendering Japanese text inside images — [WEEL](https://weel.co.jp/media/innovator/nanobanana-figure/); [WEEL usage roundup](https://weel.co.jp/media/innovator/nanobananausage/)

**A4. ChatGPT memory-based caricature, early Feb 2026**
- Prompt: "Create a caricature of me and my job based on everything you know about me" plus an uploaded photo. It spread on Instagram, X, Facebook and TikTok. Reviewers found the results only as good as the chat history, and some users were offended by inaccurate depictions. Privacy concerns were raised — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/viral-chatgpt-ai-caricature-trend-takes-over-could-it-risk-your-job-and-privacy-1.500441755); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/i-tried-the-new-chatgpt-caricature-trend-and-was-shocked-how-well-the-ai-chatbot-knows-me); [WRDW](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/02/04/what-tech-what-does-ai-really-know-about-you); [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/ai/ai-art/this-viral-ai-caricature-trend-is-everywhere-heres-how-to-make-one-in-chatgpt)
- **Japan:** Forbes JAPAN ran a translated piece on Feb 9, 2026 with the Japanese prompt 「あなたが私について知っていることすべてに基づいて、私と私の仕事の似顔絵を作成して」. It gave no Japan-specific metrics — [Forbes JAPAN](https://forbesjapan.com/articles/detail/91327). Japanese how-to blogs exist and warn about uploading photos of other people — [bloomeria](https://bloomeria.jp/blog/chatgpt-caricature-trend-guide); [hashout](https://hashout.jp/?p=383)

#### B. "Ask the AI what it thinks of me" self-analysis prompts (LLM memory as mirror)

- **[PRECEDENT, 2024→] ChatGPT Instagram roast:** users screenshot their profile or feed and ask "Roast my Instagram feed in one paragraph". Know Your Meme traces an early post to Aug 9, 2024 — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/chatgpt-instagram-roast-challenge); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/everyones-asking-chatgpt-to-roast-them-heres-how-to-try-it)
- **"What do you know about me" / "draw my life based on what you know":** a memory-based image prompt spread on Instagram. Coverage advises checking privacy settings — [VCGamers](https://www.vcgamers.com/news/en/how-to-make-what-you-know-of-me-in-chatgpt)
- **"What's the most ridiculous thing I asked you this year?" (Dec 2025):** spread on TikTok and Reddit, largely among women creators, with the bot answering in a playful confidant tone. Without memory its answers were guesses — [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/culture/tiktok-trend-chatgpt-most-ridiculous-thing-asked/)
- **"Create an image of how I treat you" (Jan 2026):** a single-line prompt with "tens of thousands of reposts on Reddit and X, millions of views on TikTok". A Reddit variant asks for "an image that reflects my maturity level based on our chats" — [NavBharat Live](https://navbharatlive.com/technology/how-does-ai-see-you-find-out-from-this-viral-trend-how-you-treated-chatgpt-1525868.html); [Expreso](https://www.expreso.ec/ciencia-y-tecnologia/el-trend-viral-que-le-pide-chatgpt-mostrar-como-lo-tratas-un-espejo-del-usuario-272008)
- **"Your Year with ChatGPT" (official, Dec 22, 2025):** a Wrapped-style recap with conversation themes, usage stats, playful "awards", and a generated poem and image. It rolled out in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand to users who had memory and chat-history referencing turned on. Team and Enterprise accounts were excluded — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/your-year-with-chatgpt-openai-launches-2025-recap-for-chatgpt-users-enn25122302667); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/openai-chatgpt-recap-2025/). TechRadar called it "more embarrassing than any song list" — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/chatgpts-attempt-at-spotify-wrapped-is-more-embarrassing-than-any-song-list)

#### C. Meme-typology quizzes (MBTI successors)

**C1. Korea: テト/エゲン (테토/에겐, Testosterone/Estrogen) test, peak summer 2025**
- Origin: popular Instagram cartoonist ネッチョ (nezzo) published a webtoon on the theory in spring 2024. Tests spread rapidly on TikTok and Instagram in 2025 among people "bored of MBTI" — [Yahoo!ニュース エキスパート, 2025-08-14](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/ff566917eb602f48f2277a566a4b44c8011325dc)
- Results: 4 types (teto man, egen man, teto woman, egen woman), shown as a ratio score (e.g., "teto 4 / egen 15") — [Yahoo!ニュース エキスパート](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/ff566917eb602f48f2277a566a4b44c8011325dc)
- Scale: over 1M people had reportedly taken teto-egen tests online by July 2025 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teto-egen_test). One 28-question test passed 630,000 participants — [Forbes JAPAN, 2025-08-06](https://forbesjapan.com/articles/detail/81078)
- Uses: dating compatibility combined with MBTI and attachment styles, and typing K-pop idols (Jungkook as テト男; TWICE Mina and LE SSERAFIM Kazuha as エゲン) — [Forbes JAPAN](https://forbesjapan.com/articles/detail/81078); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teto-egen_test)
- Commercial and culture spillover: an Ayoung FBC wine curation campaign (Aug 2025) — [CHOSUNBIZ](https://biz.chosun.com/en/en-retail/2025/08/13/UIREZA5B7BDBDKX5VD5JRPYDAQ/); an SNL Korea parody; Taiwanese teto/egen makeup guides and celebrity typing in Cosmopolitan TW and ELLE TW (July 2025) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teto-egen_test); [ELLE TW](https://www.elle.com/tw/entertainment/gossip/g65426453/teto-girl-challenge/)
- Korean framing: "MBTI 다음은 너다" ("you're next after MBTI", June 2025) — [전북일보](https://www.jjan.kr/articleAmp/20250613580066). Experts warn over-immersion harms relationships — [아시아경제](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2025061016140246779). Seoul National University and Dankook psychologists warn it reinforces gender binaries and stereotypes — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teto-egen_test)
- **Japan reach:** Japanese explainers appeared in Aug 2025 (Forbes JAPAN, Gendai, Yahoo). The Forbes columnist *speculated* it could go viral in Japan via 推し活, dating and SNS, comparing it to 草食系/肉食系 — [Forbes JAPAN](https://forbesjapan.com/articles/detail/81078); [現代ビジネス](https://gendai.media/articles/-/170757). No evidence of mass Japanese adoption was found.

**C5. Rarity calculator: "How Rare Are You?" (howrareami.org), 2026**
- Covers 35 traits across 5 categories (body, genetics, personality, life experiences, career). Each answer maps to a research statistic, and the probabilities are multiplied against 8 billion people. About 3 minutes per category. 50,000 quizzes were taken in the first 3 days, with about 160k page-view events and 2m33s average engagement. Free, with no ads yet. ### Inferences
- **Lifespan pattern:** AI-image prompt trends burn out in about 1–3 weeks and are immediately replaced by the next prompt (Ghibli in March → action figure in April → figurine in September → hug-younger-self and Ghostface in Sep–Oct → caricature the following February). Lensa's 58% revenue decline the next year is the monetization warning. A Japanese product built on "one prompt" would need a pipeline of new templates rather than one hit.
- **Who wins image trends:** in 2025–2026 the free first-party AI apps (ChatGPT, Gemini) captured the AI-image trends, because users run the prompt there at zero cost. A standalone web tool can only compete by adding something those apps lack: a fixed, branded, kawaii template; a group or pair mechanic; Japanese text on the image (which Nano Banana handles poorly); or a quiz-to-image combination.
- **Typology quizzes remain the best fit for an indie web tool.** SBTI shows that one individual's no-login page with meme-y labels can reach China-scale virality within 24 hours, at near-zero cost and with no LLM required. The success factors that recur across テトエゲン, SBTI, the NetEase H5s and 16Personalities:
  - Short completion time (10 seconds to 5 minutes).
  - A shareable label vocabulary that works as social shorthand (テト男, 吗喽).
  - Relational use (dating compatibility, idol typing).
  - A screenshot-friendly result card.
- **"Wrapped"-style reports are now expected by users** (Spotify, ChatGPT, NetEase). "Listening Age" shows that a single provocative, slightly wrong number ("your music age is 52") drives memes more than accuracy does.
- **Memory-mirror prompts ("what do you think of me") are a new 2025–2026 genre.** A web tool can replicate the feeling without ChatGPT memory by having users paste text, answer questions, or connect data. Accuracy disputes and offense are part of the share loop (people post "it got me wrong").

## Q3. Which enablers matter in 2026 and what do they cost per user?

### Takeaway
Personalized **text** generation now costs about $0.0003–$0.002 per result with nano/lite models, so an LLM-written "reading" for 1M users costs roughly $300–$1,600. That is negligible. **Image** generation costs about $0.005–$0.17 per image, so 1M images cost $5k–$170k. That gap is why the AI-image trends lived inside free first-party apps and why Lensa charged $3.99. Model churn is fast: Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (the original Nano Banana) shut down on Oct 2, 2026.

### Inferences (worked cost estimates; my calculations from the list prices above, FX ¥150/$ assumed)
- **LLM text reading.** Assumes about 1,500 input tokens (system prompt plus answers) and 600 output tokens (roughly a 1,000–1,200-character Japanese reading).
  - gpt-5-nano ≈ $0.0003
  - gemini-2.5-flash-lite ≈ $0.0004
  - Claude Haiku 5.5 ≈ $0.00045
  - gpt-5-mini ≈ $0.0016
  - **1M readings ≈ $300–$1,600 (≈ ¥45k–¥240k).** Prompt caching or batching lowers this further. Japanese text tokenizes less efficiently than English, so add a buffer.
- **AI image per user.**
  - gpt-image-1-mini low ≈ $0.005
  - Nano Banana 2.1 or flash-lite-image at 1K ≈ $0.034 (batch $0.017)
  - gpt-image-1.5 medium ≈ $0.034
  - Nano Banana Pro ≈ $0.134
  - **1M users × 1 image ≈ $5k–$134k (≈ ¥0.75M–¥20M)**, and users typically regenerate 2–4 times. A viral spike can therefore produce a 6–7-figure-yen bill within days unless the tool has rate limits, a queue, a paid "HD" upsell, ads, or a sponsor.
- **TTS:** a 300-character spoken reading on tts-1 ≈ $0.0045 per user, which makes voice result cards cheap enough for a viral free tier.
- **Architecture implication:** the cheapest viral design is a **static quiz plus deterministic result cards** (SBTI-style; near-zero marginal cost, best for 10s users) with an *optional* LLM "deep reading" layer. Make image generation a gated or limited extra rather than the core loop.

### Gaps
- No official per-image figure for gpt-image-2 or gpt-image-1.5 quality tiers was fetched directly; the 1.5 and mini numbers come from search summaries of OpenAI model pages.
- No data on face-analysis API costs (e.g., on-device vs cloud), or on Apple/Google on-device model APIs for web contexts (not researched in depth).
- No public postmortem was found with real infrastructure bills from a viral AI quiz.

## Q4. Which formats already reached Japan, which have not, and what adaptations do Japanese Gen Z (10s–20s) need?
